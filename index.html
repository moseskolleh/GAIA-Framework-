    Provenance & uncertainty

    Ranges are energy-driven bounds from the model row's data tier (×1.15–×3). Factor uncertainty — grid intensity, PUE, WUE, off-site water — adds beyond them and is not compounded into the band. Equivalents are communication aids, not results.

    What would change it

    Each line re-runs the same equations with exactly one input changed. Percentages are the change in monthly location-based carbon. A percentage here is an energy claim, never a quality claim — capability class is a coarse bucket, and the right-sizing question requires you to evaluate the smaller model on your own task before treating any of these as available.

      How to read this table

      • Wh / 1k out is energy per thousand output tokens. Input tokens are charged at one tenth of that rate.
      • Bounds are not error bars in the statistical sense — they are the plausible interval implied by the evidence tier and by disagreement between sources.
      • Tier is the provenance of the number, not the quality of the model: T1 measured, T2 provider-disclosed, T3 independently benchmarked, T4 modelled from parameter physics.
      • Active params drives energy for mixture-of-experts models; total parameters drive memory, not energy per token.
      • Rows with the same active-parameter count get the same modelled energy. That is what a physics model implies — and a standing reminder that a T4 row is an estimate, not a measurement.

      Why the open-weight rows matter

      Open-weight models can be metered directly: you can move them from T4 (modelled) to T1 (measured) with a power log, which no API model allows. They are also where the efficiency frontier moved fastest — sparse mixture-of-experts architectures serve frontier-adjacent quality at a fraction of the active parameters, and therefore a fraction of the energy per token. The database keeps both, labelled, so the trade-off is visible instead of assumed.

      Compare scenarios

      Put deployment options side by side on identical workloads. The first scenario is the baseline; the others report their difference against it. This is the decision GAIA exists to inform: not “is AI bad”, but “which of these ways of doing it costs least”.

        Set targets on intensity, not totals

        A target on absolute emissions punishes growth and rewards doing less of a useful thing. A target on intensity — Wh per request, g CO₂e per 1M tokens, L per user-month — rewards the engineering that actually reduces footprint, and it is the quantity the SCI specification defines. Report totals alongside for budget and disclosure, but grade and target the rate.

        The disclosure ask

        Most of the uncertainty in any GAIA assessment is not a limitation of the method — it is provider opacity. The single most effective long-term lever is procurement: ask vendors for per-request energy at a stated boundary, hosting region, facility PUE and WUE, and market-based emission factors. Two providers now publish audited or full-stack measured figures; the rest can be asked to.

        Method

        GAIA does not replace measurement standards — it builds on them and adds the organisational decision layer that connects them into a workflow anyone can run.

        The four modules

        G

        Ground

        Inventory AI use: models, deployment path, hosting region, volume, token profile. Declare functional units and the system boundary.

        A

        Assess

        Compute energy, dual-reported carbon and water per functional unit — always low / central / high, at the best available data tier.

        I

        Interpret

        Grade A–E within the task class, run the three-question frugality check, and place totals in context with sourced equivalents.

        A′

        Act

        Pick evidence-ranked levers, set intensity targets, and report with the SCI-compatible disclosure template.

        The provenance ladder

        T1 — Measured×/÷ 1.15

        Direct metering of your own deployment. The gold standard, and the reason open-weight models can be assessed more honestly than API ones.

        T2 — Disclosed×/÷ 1.5

        Provider-published full-stack measurement with a stated boundary — a per-prompt disclosure, or an audited life-cycle assessment.

        T3 — Benchmarked×/÷ 2

        Independent standardised benchmarks. GPU-only figures are corrected with the serving-stack multiplier before entering the database.

        T4 — Modelled×/÷ 3

        Parameter-count physics: active parameters → FLOPs → joules via accelerator efficiency and serving utilisation. Used where nobody has measured.

        Principles

        P1 · Traceable or absent

        Every number carries a source, a vintage and a tier. A value that cannot be sourced is not published — not even as a placeholder.

        P2 · Uncertainty is part of the answer

        Point estimates without bounds are pseudo-precision. Every result is an interval whose width is set by the evidence tier.

        P3 · Separate what varies independently

        Model → energy per token. Facility → overhead and on-site water. Grid → carbon and off-site water. Supply chain → embodied.

        P4 · Rates before totals

        Totals scale with usage, so they cannot judge efficiency. GAIA grades intensity per functional unit and reports totals alongside.

        P5 · Fit-for-purpose beats raw efficiency

        The greenest query is the one that used the smallest adequate model — or was never sent.

        P6 · Dual carbon reporting

        Location-based and market-based carbon answer different questions. Both are reported; they are never blended.

        P7 · Comparable by construction

        GAIA's quantities map one-to-one onto SCI variables and GHG Protocol scopes, so an assessment can be restated without rework.

        No composite score

        The pair (grade, frugality flag) is the result. Collapsing them into one number is exactly the arbitrariness GAIA exists to remove.

          Known limitations, stated plainly

          • Provider opacity. Closed providers do not disclose per-model serving data; most model rows are T3/T4 with wide bounds. That is a property of the industry, not the framework — the tier column makes the opacity itself visible.
          • Benchmark ≠ production. Benchmarks measure specific hardware and serving configurations; production fleets differ — batching, quantisation and speculative decoding shift per-token energy several-fold in either direction.
          • Annual-average carbon. Grid intensity averages ignore hourly variation; marginal and hourly emissions accounting remain out of scope.
          • Water is the weakest data. Off-site water intensity varies by basin and season, and water-stress context — where a litre is drawn — matters as much as volume.
          • Reasoning tokens are partly invisible. Where a provider hides its reasoning tokens, the token count you enter understates the work done. Use the reasoning task class rather than the visible output length.
          • Rebound effects — efficiency leading to more usage — are real and unmodelled. The frugality flag is the partial control.
